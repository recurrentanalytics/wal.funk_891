02 · The places

Two microphones in the Pacific

One sits 891 metres down on a ridge off Monterey Bay and has recorded almost without a break since 2015. The other is a few metres under the surface in Haro Strait, where the Southern Resident orcas pass.

04 · The test

Can a model hear what people hear?

Before anything listens to new audio, it has to pass a test on recordings people have already labelled: 462 five-second windows, 119 with an orca call. The pass mark, set in advance, is a balanced accuracy of 0.80.

How Perch hears it

The sound map

Every dot is one 5-second window. Perch turns each into 1,536 numbers; this map squeezes them into two dimensions so that windows that sound alike land close together. Nobody told Perch which windows hold calls, yet the amber calls gather on their own.

      06 · Words

      What the terms mean

      Hydrophone
      An underwater microphone.
      Spectrogram
      A picture of sound: time left to right, pitch bottom to top, loudness as brightness.
      Window
      A 5-second slice of audio. Models judge one window at a time; windows overlap by half.
      Embedding
      The 1,536 numbers Perch makes from a window. Windows that sound alike get similar numbers.
      Classifier
      A small model trained on labelled examples that turns an embedding into one score from 0 to 1.
      Threshold
      The score at which a window counts as a call. Fixed at 0.5 before testing and never moved to pass a test.
      Balanced accuracy
      The average of calls found and quiet windows left alone. 0.5 is guessing, 1.0 is perfect.
      Gate
      A pass mark set before a step runs. A miss stops the work until the owner decides.
      Throttling
      The Pi slowing itself down above 85 °C to protect the chip.
      Candidate
      An unusual detection. It only becomes a finding once a person has listened and checked what MBARI and Orcasound already report.
